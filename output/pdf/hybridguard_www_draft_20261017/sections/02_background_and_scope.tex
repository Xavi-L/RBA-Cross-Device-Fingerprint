\hypertarget{sec:2-1}{%
\subsection{Fingerprint Consistency}\label{sec:2-1}}

Fingerprinting research examines relationships as well as individual attributes. Rendered Private identifies rendering discrepancies exploited by WebGL fingerprinting \cite{ref17}. FP-Scanner detects countermeasure-induced inconsistencies, including discrepancies across JavaScript and HTTP reports \cite{ref04}. Picasso compares canvas challenge responses against device-class expectations \cite{ref05}. FP-Inconsistent studies spatial and temporal inconsistencies in evasive bot traffic \cite{ref06}. BrowserFM models dependencies among fingerprints, system characteristics, and browser configurations \cite{ref07}. Our study builds on this use of consistency as evidence.

WebView research examines both sides of the application--Web boundary. WVProfiler studies fingerprint exposure through Android WebView instrumentation \cite{ref08}; Tracking Without Borders examines tracking across Java/Kotlin, JavaScript, and runtime behavior \cite{ref09}; Cross-Boundary Mobile Tracking studies Java-to-JavaScript information diffusion \cite{ref10}. Our focus is a current Web report's compatibility with associated Android device and container measurements, including relation applicability, missing evidence, and behavior under edits and legitimate changes. \hypertarget{sec:2-2}{%
\subsection{Surfaces and Compatibility}\label{sec:2-2}}

Let \(E\) denote the execution state and container context. An observation \(x = (N(E), H(E), W(E))\) associates Native system and hardware measurements, WebView Host context, and App Web reports. The App schema has 177 catalogued fields: 84 Native, 26 Host, and 67 App Web. An optional Browser contributes 67 fields from a separate Web execution, yielding a 244-field paired schema when validly associated. Selected detectors use subsets of these fields. Association does not imply atomic sampling, and no surface is trusted ground truth. Figure~\ref{fig:surfaces} summarizes the observation surfaces and their evaluation roles.

A semantic relation \(C_j\) constrains observations within a stated applicability domain. A local edit may replace \(W(E)\) with \(W^{\prime}(E)\) while a reference stays unchanged; a legitimate change may shift related observations together. Compatibility accounts for differences in API units, precision, and abstractions. The notation \(E\) describes the execution context; checks use the collected measurements.

A violation of an applicable relation provides \textbf{conditional report-integrity evidence}: the observed values conflict with the declared constraint. The evidence identifies a relational inconsistency, while attribution to an altered operand and the underlying true values remain unresolved. In evaluation, a trigger on a known effective modification counts as a detection.

\hypertarget{sec:2-3}{%
\subsection{Adversary and Claim Boundary}\label{sec:2-3}}

We use controlled browser overrides or emulation to edit resource, browser, locale, display, and graphics reports exposed to the Web application while a compared reference may remain unchanged. An operation counts as effective only when its effect is observed at the target surface. At inference, the detector evaluates each current record using its associated observations and the checks selected offline.

The intended setting is a cooperating Android application that collects Native and Host measurements alongside its embedded webpage. An ordinary remote website needs such integration to obtain these observations. Native and Host measurements are themselves mutable; being outside a local Web override does not make them administratively or statistically independent.

An ALERT indicates a violation of a selected relation, whereas NO ALERT indicates definite non-violation across all selected checks. Both describe the evaluated relations under available evidence, leaving attacker intent and authenticity unresolved. Local or coordinated edits may preserve these relations. We study controlled Web-report modifications under the stated collector and backend assumptions; collector or backend compromise, adaptive evasion, tracking, and device re-identification fall beyond this evaluation.
