\section{Conclusion}
This study examines how cross-layer semantic consistency can provide integrity evidence for Web-visible environment reports in Android hybrid Web applications. HybridGuard associates complementary Native, WebView Host, and App Web observations from the same execution, implements semantic relations as checks with explicit applicability conditions, and studies them through controlled manipulations and normal changes. The Native memory comparison and standalone Host geometry study show that device and container references supply context absent from isolated Web checks, exposing some otherwise missed modifications. Interpreting legitimate system and layout changes also avoids specific normal alarms in the tested comparisons while retaining detection.

These benefits depend on references that provide distinguishing context, relations with defined semantics and applicability, and available evidence. Detection benefits must be assessed alongside normal alarms and inconclusive outcomes; absence of an alarm does not confirm an unmodified environment. Consistency establishes no trusted root, and coordinated modifications preserving checked relations may evade detection. Under the studied conditions, cross-layer semantic consistency therefore offers useful, conditional integrity evidence for Web-visible environment reports.
