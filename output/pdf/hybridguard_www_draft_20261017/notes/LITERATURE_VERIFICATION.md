## Extra official context

[W3C Privacy Working Group, Mitigating Browser Fingerprinting in Web Specifications](https://www.w3.org/TR/fingerprinting-guidance/) is a **Group Note dated 25 September 2025**, not a W3C Recommendation or a standard that certifies individual detector predicates. It supports discussing privacy costs, minimizing exposed information, and graceful degradation.

## Positioning and current-evidence boundary

The defensible distinction is the study of semantic consistency across Android Native, the WebView host, and the in-app Web runtime, with explicit field states, source-specific relations, and controlled baseline/active evidence. Prior work already covers inconsistency detection, cross-browser identification, and WebView information flow. Keep any first claim scoped to the explicit three-observation formulation and qualified by the literature reviewed; do not claim universal anti-spoofing or production fraud detection.

## Scoped novelty clarification (18 October 2026)

The abstract now retains: "To the best of our knowledge, HybridGuard is the first framework to explicitly formulate mobile fingerprint manipulation detection as consistency checking across Android Native, the WebView host, and in-app Web observations."

The focused comparison distinguishes [Fp-Scanner](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity18/presentation/vastel), which detects alterations through browser-fingerprint inconsistencies; [Android WebView Fingerprinting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.01968), which studies WebView fingerprinting and information leakage; and [WebViewTracer](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/cross-boundary-mobile-tracking-exploring-java-to-javascript-information-diffusion-in-webviews/), which studies Java/JavaScript tracking diffusion. Their stated contributions do not establish the same explicit three-observation manipulation-check formulation. This bounded review supports articulating the specific novelty claim but does not prove that no prior work exists globally.

The first claim concerns framework formulation. Host--Web geometry remains a separate specialist study, the Native--Host edge is contextual, and 105/126 is not a result for an integrated detector jointly using every three-way relation. The text does not claim first fingerprint inconsistency detection or a formal theoretical guarantee.
